1. Power architecture

File: 01_power_architecture.svg

This is the single most important diagram. Two separate power sources feed the arm:

  1. The laptop's USB supplies the Mega (5 V at up to ~500 mA, plenty for the microcontroller).
  2. The 4×AA pack supplies the four servos (≈ 6 V at several amps peak).

The two supplies share one node: ground. Without that common return path, the servo signal from the Mega and the servo's own reference voltage disagree, and the servos twitch or refuse to respond.

Never do this The battery pack's + must not touch the Mega's 5V pin, VIN, or any red rail that carries USB 5 V. You'd be shorting two supplies with different voltages — best case, the USB host cuts power; worst case, something smokes.

2. HC-05 voltage divider

File: 02_hc05_voltage_divider.svg

Both Arduinos in this project output 5 V logic. The HC-05's RX input is rated for 3.3 V. Feeding 5 V straight in works for a while, then kills the module.

The fix is a resistive divider between the Arduino TX pin and the HC-05 RX pin:

  1. R1 = 1 kΩ, in series, between Arduino TX and the junction.
  2. R2 = 2 kΩ, from the junction down to GND.
  3. The HC-05 RX pin connects at the junction itself.

The voltage at the junction is V_out = V_in × R2 / (R1 + R2) = 5 V × 2 / (1 + 2) ≈ 3.33 V — exactly what the HC-05 wants. If you want belt-and-braces, a proper 3.3 V level shifter IC (TXS0108E, BSS138) is bulletproof and no arithmetic to get wrong.

3. Arm side — full wiring

File: 03_arm_wiring.svg

Everything that lives on the arm base. Follow the coloured wires:

  1. Red — 6 V from battery+ up to each servo V+ (shared rail).
  2. Dark — GND. Battery−, all four servo GNDs, and Mega GND all meet at one junction (the common-ground node highlighted on the diagram).
  3. Amber — servo PWM signal wires. D9 → base, D10 → shoulder, D11 → elbow, D6 → claw.
  4. Blue — TX1 (D18) from Mega into the divider, then on to HC-05 RXD.
  5. Green — TXD from HC-05 straight into Mega RX1 (D19). No divider needed because 3.3 V is read as HIGH by the Mega's 5 V input.
Bulk capacitor A 220–470 µF electrolytic across the servo V+/GND rail, close to the servos, prevents brown-outs when all four accelerate at once. Long leg to +, short leg to −. This is a quick win to make the arm move smoothly under the 4×AA pack.

4. Controller side — full wiring

File: 04_controller_wiring.svg

The handheld controller has three things attached to the Uno R4 Minima: the joystick, the HC-05 master, and a USB cable (to your laptop during development; later, a power bank or 9 V battery in the Vin jack).

Upload gotcha — worth reading twice The HC-05 TXD wire on D0 blocks USB uploads. Every time you want to re-flash the Uno R4, pop that single jumper off D0, upload, and put it back. The D1 side can stay wired.

Joystick mapping in firmware: VRx → A0, VRy → A1, SW → D2 (with internal pull-up). If an axis feels backwards once you're moving the arm, flip the sign of jx or jy in controller_uno_r4/main.cpp.
